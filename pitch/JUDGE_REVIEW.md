## What changed after feedback

The narration now specifies a scenario-planning workflow, the local/backend timing split and candidate count separately from demand-zone count. The business section names an operator planning team and one retrospective pilot. The demo sequence proves recalculation and stale-result exclusion. Primary sources support the clinical and payment context, with the Comuna 4 curation disclosed.

We answered the architecture question with the actual case → shared coverage → greedy/exact or Dicke/XY/local tuning → one backend job → bit decoding/validity/true coverage → revision-checked UI path. We did not add fictitious operator interviews, contracts, proprietary technology, revenue, cost savings or quantum advantages.

## Why the requested 5/5 threshold was not met

Thiel scored monopoly, contrarian differentiation and zero-to-one commercial potential. Galperin emphasized customer validation, distribution and defensibility. Torvalds emphasized production architecture and resource efficiency. Their central objections were missing operator demand and a missing benefit of quantum over strong classical alternatives.

Several AI comments conflated more census zones with a larger candidate problem, or treated a quantum experiment without proven advantage as a contradiction. Follow-up answers corrected that. Other criticisms are material: the public prototype has no validated buyer, willingness to pay, production deployment or quantum superiority.

These personas continued to evaluate a venture even after the quantum-impact hackathon scope was explained. Their rating is one rehearsal signal, not the competition's rubric. We did not replace them with permissive custom judges, ask for fabricated five-star verdicts, or hide low scores.

## Evidence that could improve the commercial assessment

1. One real operator interview identifying a costly, specific planning decision and its current tools.
2. A data-access or pilot-interest commitment from that planning owner.
3. Held-out comparisons against the actual current plan and competitive classical methods, with feasible fleet/road constraints.
4. Measured willingness to pay and integration/support cost.
5. Larger-candidate quantum results that justify their total resource cost, if quantum is presented as the competitive differentiator.

The pitch/demo kit is ready to rehearse. **The all-judges 5/5 requirement remains unmet** on the available evidence.
